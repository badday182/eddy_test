# Topic: Nuclear Fission Reaction

## General Overview:

**Nuclear fission** is a nuclear reaction in which a heavy nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei after absorbing a neutron.

**Mechanism of the phenomenon:**
*   **Deformation:** A neutron impact causes the nucleus to stretch.
*   **Fission (Splitting):** As protons move apart, electrostatic repulsive forces overcome the strong nuclear interaction holding them together, and the nucleus tears apart.

**Energy and Products:**
*   As a result, lighter elements (fission fragments) are formed, and several free neutrons are released.
*   **Mass Defect:** The total mass of the fission products is less than the mass of the initial particles. This difference is converted into a colossal amount of energy according to the formula **\(E=mc^2\)**.

**Chain Reaction:**
The released neutrons can trigger the fission of other nuclei, creating a self-sustaining reaction. For stable energy release in reactors, this process is regulated using control rods that absorb excess neutrons.

## Detailed Description:

### **Definition of the Phenomenon**
**Nuclear fission** is the process of splitting a heavy atomic nucleus (such as uranium-235) into two or more smaller nuclei, accompanied by the release of free neutrons and a significant amount of energy.

### **Physical Mechanism of the Process**
1.  **Interplay of Forces in the Nucleus:** Inside a stable nucleus, two opposing forces act: the **strong nuclear interaction**, which holds protons and neutrons together at very short distances, and the **electrostatic force**, which causes repulsion between positively charged protons.
2.  **Deformation of the Nucleus:** The process is initiated when a high-velocity **neutron** strikes the nucleus of a heavy isotope. This leads to the deformation (stretching) of the nucleus.
3.  **Fission (Splitting):** As the nucleus stretches, protons at opposite ends move to a distance where the strong nuclear interaction becomes ineffective due to its short range. At this point, electrostatic repulsion predominates, tearing the nucleus apart.

### **Reaction Products and Energy Yield**
*   **Fission Fragments:** Two lighter nuclei are formed (for example, barium-142 and krypton-91).
*   **Free Neutrons:** During fission, several neutrons are released (typically three in the case of uranium-235).
*   **Energy Release:** The total mass of the fission products is less than the mass of the original nucleus and neutron. This difference (mass defect) is converted into energy according to the mass-energy equivalence principle **\(E=mc^2\)**. Energy is released in the form of heat and gamma radiation.

### **Chain Reaction and Control**
*   **Chain Reaction Principle:** Neutrons released during the fission of one nucleus can hit neighboring nuclei, causing them to split. This creates a self-sustaining process whose intensity rapidly increases.
*   **Critical Mass:** For a steady chain reaction to occur, a certain minimum amount of material—critical mass—is required, at which neutrons have enough time to hit other nuclei before escaping the volume of material.
*   **Process Regulation:** In nuclear reactors, the reaction rate is controlled using **control rods** made of materials that absorb excess neutrons. This allows maintaining the reaction at a stable level and preventing uncontrollable energy release.
