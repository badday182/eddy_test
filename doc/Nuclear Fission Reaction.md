# Topic: Nuclear Fission Reaction

## General Overview:

**Nuclear fission** is a nuclear reaction in which a heavy nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei after absorbing a neutron.

**Mechanism of the phenomenon:**
*   **Overlapping State & Separation:** The heavy Uranium-235 nucleus is composed of two overlapping constituent atomic nuclei: Barium-142 ($^{142}_{56}\text{Ba}$) and Krypton-91 ($^{91}_{36}\text{Kr}$). Upon absorbing a neutron, the two overlapping nuclei slide apart from their merged state, as electrostatic Coulomb repulsive forces overcome the short-range strong nuclear interaction and push them apart in opposite directions.

**Energy and Products:**
*   As a result, two lighter elements (fission fragments: Barium-142 and Krypton-91) recede from each other, **3 prompt free neutrons ($3 \times n^0$)** (represented in 3D as 3 bright glowing yellow spheres) fly apart in 3 different 3D vectors, and high-frequency **gamma radiation ($\gamma$-rays)** propagates outward as electromagnetic sine waves.
*   **Mass Defect:** The total mass of the fission products is less than the mass of the initial particles ($\Delta m \approx 0.2154\,\text{u}$). This difference is converted into a colossal amount of energy according to the formula **\(E=\Delta m \cdot c^2 \approx 200\,\text{MeV} = 3.2 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{J}\)**.

**Chain Reaction:**
The released neutrons can trigger the fission of neighboring nuclei, creating a self-sustaining reaction. For stable energy release in reactors, this process is regulated using control rods that absorb excess neutrons.

### **2. Physical Mechanism of the Process**
1.  **Interplay of Forces in the Nucleus:** Inside a stable nucleus, two opposing forces act: the **strong nuclear interaction** ($F_{\text{strong}}$), which holds protons and neutrons together at very short distances ($\sim 1\text{--}2\,\text{fm}$), and the **electrostatic Coulomb force** ($F_{\text{coulomb}}$), which causes repulsion between positively charged protons.
2.  **Overlapping Nuclei Model & Separation Initiation:** The Uranium-235 nucleus is structured as two constituent overlapping atomic clusters ($^{142}_{56}\text{Ba}$ and $^{91}_{36}\text{Kr}$). Upon absorbing a thermal neutron, compound $^{236}\text{U}^*$ receives excitation energy, causing the two overlapping atomic structures of Barium and Krypton to slide apart from their merged state at the center.
3.  **Complete Separation & Energy Bloom:** As the two atomic nuclei move beyond the range of the strong nuclear force, electrostatic Coulomb repulsion predominates, accelerating Barium-142 and Krypton-91 apart in opposite directions and releasing $\sim 200\,\text{MeV}$ of kinetic and radiant energy ($E=mc^2$).
4.  **Free Neutrons & Gamma Wave Propagation:**
    *   **3 Free Yellow Neutrons ($n^0$):** Ejected immediately during the separation into 3 different 3D trajectories.
    *   **Gamma Radiation ($\gamma$-rays):** Propagates outward as **transverse sine wave energy rays** ($y = A \sin(k \cdot r - \omega \cdot t)$) in 8 radial 3D directions at light speed.

---

### **3. Reaction Products and Energy Yield**
*   **Fission Fragments:** Two lighter atomic nuclei ($^{142}_{56}\text{Ba}$ and $^{91}_{36}\text{Kr}$) receding from each other due to Coulomb repulsion.
*   **Prompt Free Neutrons:** 3 bright glowing yellow spheres ($3 \times n^0$) released into space.
*   **Energy Release & Mass Defect:** 
    $$\Delta m = m_{\text{U-235}} + m_n - (m_{\text{Ba-142}} + m_{\text{Kr-91}} + 3m_n) = 0.2154\,\text{u}$$
    $$E = \Delta m \cdot c^2 \approx 200\,\text{MeV} = 3.2 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{Joules per fission}$$

---

### **4. Chain Reaction and Control**
*   **Chain Reaction Principle:** Neutrons released during the fission of one nucleus can hit neighboring $^{235}\text{U}$ nuclei in a fuel grid, causing a self-sustaining process.
*   **Critical Multiplication Factor ($k_{\text{eff}}$):**
    *   **Subcritical ($k_{\text{eff}} < 1.0$):** Reaction dies out (control rods inserted).
    *   **Critical ($k_{\text{eff}} = 1.0$):** Stable energy output (50% control rod position).
    *   **Supercritical ($k_{\text{eff}} > 1.0$):** Exponential growth (control rods withdrawn).
*   **Process Regulation:** Controllable Boron/Cadmium control rods absorb excess neutrons to maintain steady power generation.
